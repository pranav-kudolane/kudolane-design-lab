A soft glow that tracks the cursor across the button. Micro-interactions like this lift perceived quality fast. Follow the pointer with JS.
// track pointer inside the button

03 — Accessibility that ships

Make it beautiful and usable by everyone

These are the checks that catch ~90% of real issues — and they protect you legally while widening your clients' audience. Run through them before every handoff.

🎨Color & Contrast

  • Body text hits 4.5:1 contrast, large text 3:1 (WCAG AA). Check every glass/gradient overlay.
  • Never signal meaning by color alone — add icons, labels or underlines (errors, links, status).
  • Test your palette in a color-blind simulator; ~8% of men can't distinguish red/green.

⌨️Keyboard & Focus

  • Every interactive element is reachable by Tab and usable without a mouse.
  • Keep a visible focus ring — style :focus-visible, never outline:none alone.
  • Logical tab order that follows the visual layout; add a “skip to content” link.

🏗️Structure & Semantics

  • Use real tags — <button>, <nav>, <main> — not <div onclick>.
  • One H1 per page; headings nested in order (don't skip H2→H4).
  • Every image has meaningful alt text (or alt="" if purely decorative).

🎬Motion & Media

  • Honor prefers-reduced-motion — kill big animations for users who need calm.
  • No pure autoplay audio; give controls. Captions/transcripts for video.
  • Avoid content that flashes >3×/sec (seizure risk).

📝Forms & Touch

  • Every input has a real <label> — placeholders aren't labels.
  • Tap targets ≥ 44×44px with breathing room between them.
  • Clear, specific error messages tied to the field, announced to screen readers.

🔍Verify It

  • Run Lighthouse (Chrome DevTools) & the axe extension on every page.
  • Actually tab through the page and try a screen reader (VoiceOver / NVDA) once.
  • Zoom to 200% — layout must not break or clip content.
04 — Hassle-free build with AI

A workflow that keeps AI from fighting you

How to actually ship faster with AI instead of debugging its guesses. The pattern: give it constraints, build in slices, and let it do the tedious parts.

01

Feed it a design system first

Hand over your tokens up front — the exact hex palette, fonts, spacing scale, radius. AI defaults to generic looks; pinned tokens make every component consistent and on-brand.

02

Build in slices, not one shot

Ask for one component at a time — nav, then hero, then cards. Small scopes mean fewer bugs, easier review, and you can course-correct before it compounds.

03

Iterate with screenshots

Paste a screenshot and say “this spacing is off, tighten it.” Visual feedback beats paragraphs of description — a picture fixes in one turn what words take five to.

04

Delegate the boring 80%

Boilerplate, responsive breakpoints, ARIA labels, alt text, form validation, dummy content, README. Let AI grind the tedium; you spend your time on taste and logic.

05

Make it your a11y auditor

Paste a component and ask it to flag contrast, missing labels, and keyboard traps. A fast second pass that catches what you'd miss at 2am before a deadline.

06

Be specific or get generic

“Make it modern” → mush. “Glassmorphic cards, 20px blur, indigo→pink gradient, Bricolage headings” → exactly what you pictured. Name effects, sizes, and references.

07

Commit before big changes

Use Git checkpoints so an AI refactor that goes sideways is one git reset away. Freedom to experiment without fear of losing working code.

08

Always read before you ship

AI can hallucinate APIs and subtly break logic. Understand every line you hand a client — you're the one supporting it. Test the real thing, don't trust the demo.
